# yes, prepare the page-by-page copy skeleton and design spec.

Absolutely — below is a page-by-page **copy skeleton and design spec** for the website, aligned to your brief, your research story, and the four-page narrative.[^1][^2]

## Global direction

The website should feel like an **executive-grade research narrative** rather than a startup landing page. The design needs to balance drama on the first page with clarity and credibility on the later pages, while keeping the information simple enough to scan and strong enough to persuade enterprise readers.[^2]

### Global design rules

- Use a premium, restrained visual system with strong typography and dark, calm surfaces.[^2]
- Avoid generic AI visuals such as neon gradients, floating blobs, and cliché “future tech” motifs.[^2]
- Use motion lightly; the website should feel composed, not theatrical.[^2]
- Navigation should be persistent, simple, and clear across all pages.[^2]
- No login in V1; include a light contact / register-interest CTA only.[^3][^4]


### Global navigation

Top nav items:

- The Gap
- Research
- Artefacts
- Frameworks
- Contact[^2]

If you implement as one long site instead of four separate HTML pages, use these as anchored sections; if multi-page, keep the same labels for clarity.[^2]

## Page 1 — The Beast and the Gap

### Purpose

Create emotional and conceptual tension. The visitor should immediately understand that enterprise AI adoption is difficult because AI behaves probabilistically while organisations demand deterministic control.[^2]

### Design mood

- Dark, cinematic, high contrast.[^2]
- Minimal but bold.[^2]
- One striking hero visual concept: “the beast in the harness” or “powerful force straining against rails.”[^2]
- Motion, if used, should be subtle: drifting signals, unstable particles, rails under tension.[^2]


### Section 1 — Hero

**Headline:**
**AI is powerful. Organisations need control.**[^2]

**Subheading:**
Generative AI behaves probabilistically, but enterprises are built on accountability, repeatability, and traceability. That mismatch is why so many organisations struggle to move from excitement to safe adoption.[^2]

**Support line:**
A research journey into how enterprises can govern the beast without killing its value.[^2]

**CTA:**

- See the research
- Explore the artefacts[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Full-screen hero.
- Big heading, restrained copy.
- Split visual tension: organic / unstable on one side, rigid / structured on the other.
- Primary CTA prominent, secondary CTA quieter.


### Section 2 — Why organisations falter

**Heading:**
Why AI adoption stalls[^4][^2]

**Copy:**
Organisations are not irrationally resisting AI. They are built to answer hard questions: who made the decision, based on what, under which policy, and with what accountability. Generative AI breaks those assumptions by being opaque, variable, and non-deterministic.[^2]

**Callout bullets:**

- Outputs may differ for the same prompt.[^2]
- Confidence is often implicit, not explicit.[^2]
- Reasoning is difficult to inspect.[^2]
- Compliance boundaries are not naturally enforced.[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Two-column section.
- Left: enterprise expectations.
- Right: AI behaviours.
- Contrast styling with clean cards or panels.


### Section 3 — The mismatch

**Heading:**
The real problem is the gap[^2]

**Copy:**
This is not just a technology adoption challenge. It is a structural mismatch between probabilistic systems and deterministic institutions. That gap is where governance friction, stalled pilots, and failed enterprise scaling begin.[^2]

**Highlighted question:**
How can organisations safely adopt Generative AI when its inherently non-deterministic behaviour conflicts with governed, rule-bound, auditable enterprise structures?[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Centered narrow text block.
- A visual “chasm” or bridge motif beneath.
- Research question styled as a pull quote.


### Section 4 — Two ways emerged

**Heading:**
Two modes of enterprise AI[^2]

**Copy intro:**
The research showed that organisations need two different ways of working with AI, not one.[^2]

**Left card — Transformative**
Small teams, rapid experimentation, AI as colleague, new products and new operator experiences.[^2]

**Right card — Improvement**
Governed deployment in repeatable workflows, bounded risk, auditability, and measurable operational value.[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Two asymmetrical cards.
- Transformative side more fluid and open.
- Improvement side more gridded and structured.


### Section 5 — The bridge

**Heading:**
A path is needed between them[^5][^2]

**Copy:**
Transformative ideas cannot stay in the lab forever, and operational environments cannot absorb ungoverned experiments. Organisations need a transition path that establishes reliability, governance fit, regulatory readiness, and operational ownership over time.[^5][^2]

**Stage strip:**
Lab → Validated Prototype → Controlled Pilot → Hub-Spoke Deployment → Continuous Improvement[^5][^2]

**Bridge CTA:**
See how the research was done[^1][^2]

**Design spec:**

- Horizontal stage path with milestone nodes.
- Calm preview of the framework style used later.
- End with a visual transition into Page 2.

## Page 2 — Research Through DSRM

### Purpose

Turn tension into legitimacy. This page should show that the work was investigated systematically through design science, not argued abstractly.[^1][^2]

### Design mood

- Cleaner, more editorial, more structured than Page 1.[^1]
- Use lighter spacing and more visual order.[^2]
- Introduce a research aesthetic: diagrams, timelines, structured cards.[^1]


### Section 1 — Page intro

**Heading:**
Research through design science[^1]

**Subheading:**
This work used Design Science Research Methodology to investigate the enterprise AI gap through artefact creation, demonstration, and evaluation.[^1][^2]

**Design spec:**

- Strong page opener with a compact DSRM step strip underneath.
- Minimal visual noise.


### Section 2 — Why DSRM

**Heading:**
Why this methodology[^1]

**Copy:**
The problem was practical, organisational, and technical all at once. It required more than theory, but more rigour than a simple prototype exercise. DSRM provided the structure to define the problem, build artefacts, demonstrate them, evaluate them, and communicate the resulting knowledge.[^1][^2]

**Design spec:**

- Left text, right 6-step process visual.
- Each step can be a small label, not a giant block.


### Section 3 — The research question

**Heading:**
The research question[^2]

**Copy:**
How can organisations safely adopt Generative AI when its inherently non-deterministic behaviour is in direct tension with the governed, rule-bound, and auditable structures that enterprises must maintain?[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Centered quote treatment.
- Elegant typography, lots of whitespace.


### Section 4 — Two artefacts, two build modes

**Heading:**
Two artefacts were built to investigate the problem[^2]

**Intro copy:**
Rather than looking for one universal AI pattern, the research explored the problem from two directions.[^2]

**GridSense block:**
GridSense explored the transformative mode: an AI-augmented operator experience built quickly through open experimentation, short iteration cycles, and AI-assisted “vibe coding.”[^2]

**OsciProbity block:**
OsciProbity explored the governed mode: a structured, requirement-led prototype focused on architecture, specifications, traceability, and enterprise control.[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Two large feature blocks, one on each side.
- Use contrasting visual treatments to reflect different build modes.


### Section 5 — What the artefacts revealed

**Heading:**
What the research uncovered[^2]

**Copy:**
GridSense showed how AI can unlock new possibilities quickly when uncertainty is treated as part of discovery. OsciProbity showed that enterprise-ready capability requires architecture, design discipline, explicit control points, and structured development even when AI is generating code.[^2]

**Three takeaway cards:**

- Enterprises need two AI modes.[^2]
- They need a path between them.[^5][^2]
- They need governance and engineering disciplines that translate policy into working controls.[^6][^7][^8]


### Section 6 — From artefacts to frameworks

**Heading:**
From prototypes to reusable frameworks[^7][^8][^6][^5]

**Copy:**
The deeper outcome of the research was not just two prototypes. It was a set of reusable frameworks distilled from the tensions, decisions, and patterns that emerged during design, demonstration, and evaluation.[^1][^2]

**Mini framework previews:**

- Enterprise AI Framework.[^5]
- Hourglass Model.[^7]
- Guardrails Framework.[^6]
- AI Engineering Disciplines Framework.[^8]

**CTA:**
See the built artefacts[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Four compact preview cards with image thumbnails or labeled placeholders.
- This section should transition naturally into Page 3.

## Page 3 — Built Artefacts

### Purpose

Show that the research produced real, tangible systems. This page is where credibility comes from engineering, demos, and delivery detail.[^2]

### Design mood

- Product case-study feel.[^2]
- Clean and slightly more technical.
- Use screenshots, video, architecture panels, and method summaries.[^2]


### Section 1 — Page intro

**Heading:**
Built to test the capability in practice[^2]

**Subheading:**
Two prototypes were created to test how AI could support both transformative discovery and governed enterprise improvement.[^2]

### Section 2 — GridSense

**Heading:**
GridSense — transformative prototype[^2]

**Copy:**
GridSense explored what becomes possible when AI is used as a creative development partner. It was built in an open, exploratory style to rapidly imagine and test a modern, AI-augmented operator experience over legacy grid monitoring contexts.[^2]

**Feature bullets:**

- AI-augmented monitoring experience[^2]
- rapid prototype delivery[^2]
- exploratory build mode[^2]
- transformative user experience hypothesis[^2]

**Media block:**
Embedded GridSense demo video.[^2]

**Tooling strip:**
AI coding models, front-end stack, rapid iteration workflow.[^2]

**Design spec:**

- Large video/screenshot on one side, text on the other.
- More visually expressive than OsciProbity section.


### Section 3 — OsciProbity

**Heading:**
OsciProbity — governed capability prototype[^2]

**Copy:**
OsciProbity explored how a high-governance enterprise capability could be built using structured, AI-assisted development. Its emphasis was on architecture, specifications, traceability, and confidence-aware decision logic suitable for enterprise scrutiny.[^2]

**Feature bullets:**

- structured specification-led development[^2]
- architecture-first design[^2]
- deterministic algorithm ensemble for confidence scoring[^2]
- auditability, traceability, and fallback logic[^2]

**Media block:**
Embedded OsciProbity demo video.[^2]

**Design spec:**

- More architectural and gridded layout.
- Use diagram feel, perhaps with small method/process cards.


### Section 4 — How they were built

**Heading:**
AI-assisted development in two modes[^2]

**Copy:**
The artefacts were not built in the same way. GridSense used free-form, exploratory AI-assisted development to discover what a new operator experience could look like. OsciProbity used a more structured process in which specifications were created, reviewed, refined, and then used to generate code with AI tools, with review focused on functionality and low-level design coherence rather than manual line-by-line inspection.[^2]

**Method contrast table:**

- Build mode
- Team shape
- Role of AI
- Role of specs
- Role of architecture
- Review style[^2]


### Section 5 — Technology footprint

**Heading:**
Technology footprint[^2]

**Copy:**
This section should list the technology environment and tools used across the artefacts and website.[^2]

**Suggested categories:**

- AI coding tools
- Backend/services
- Data/storage
- Frontend/UI
- Testing and iteration
- Website build approach[^2]

**CTA:**
Explore the frameworks[^8][^6][^7][^5]

**Design spec:**

- Use concise technical cards or badges.
- Keep this section clean and scan-friendly.
